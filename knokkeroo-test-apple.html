Apple Watch

Live — rides along inside the iPhone app, public at 5.7.45

Before you start: your Apple Watch must already be paired to your iPhone in the normal way, and be on watchOS 9 or later. That is the Watch's own setup and has nothing to do with knokkeroo. The Watch app is free on every plan — you do not need a paid subscription for it.

Step 1 · Get knokkeroo onto the iPhone

There is no separate Watch app to install and no Watch beta — the Watch app is inside the iPhone one. The public App Store app is enough: install knokkeroo 5.7.45 from the App Store and the Watch app comes with it.

Get knokkeroo on the App Store

You only need the steps below if you would rather run the TestFlight build. It is currently the same version as the App Store (build 73, 5.7.45), so it gains you nothing today. Both buttons must be tapped on the iPhone, not on a computer.

Install TestFlight

Apple's own free app. Skip this if you already have it.

Join the knokkeroo beta

Then tap Accept and Install. A public link — nobody needs to invite you. Button did nothing? Open TestFlight, tap Redeem, and type XaabanK9.

Step 2 · Get it onto the Watch

  1. Usually it installs itself within a few minutes. Check your Watch's app list first.
  2. If it has not: on the iPhone open the Watch app → My Watch tab → scroll to Available Apps → tap Install next to knokkeroo.

Step 3 · Pairing — there is none

There is no code to type and nothing to pair. The Watch app has no login screen: it reads your signed-in session from the iPhone. So all you do is open knokkeroo on your iPhone once after installing. Then open it on the Watch and you will already be signed in.

If the Watch says you are not signed in: open the app on your iPhone, confirm you are signed in there, then open the Watch app again.

What to test

  • Watch shows your current status on open
  • Tapping knok on the Watch records a check-in
  • The iPhone app reflects a Watch-recorded knok without needing a manual refresh
  • Behaviour when signed out (should show a clear "not signed in" state, not crash)

Apple TV

Beta only — separate TestFlight app, build 68 (5.3.6). Not on the App Store

Before you start: you need an Apple TV on tvOS 16 or later, and an iPhone that already has knokkeroo installed and signed in. knokkeroo TV is free on every plan. This is a separate app from the iPhone one, with its own beta — joining the iPhone beta does not give you this.

Step 1 · Join the TV beta — on your iPhone, not on the TV

This step cannot be done on the Apple TV, and it cannot be done on a computer. A join link only works inside the TestFlight app, and the Apple TV has no way to open a web link. On a PC or Mac browser the page simply tells you to open it on a device — there is no Accept button. Do it on the iPhone; you are joining with your Apple ID, not with a particular device, which is why the TV picks it up afterwards.

Install TestFlight

Apple's own free app. Skip this if you already have it.

Join the knokkeroo TV beta

Then tap Accept. Button did nothing? Open TestFlight, tap Redeem, and type 72H1DdnT.

Make sure the iPhone is signed into the same Apple ID as the Apple TV — that is what carries the beta across.

Step 2 · Install on the Apple TV

  1. On the Apple TV, open TestFlight (install it from the tvOS App Store if it is not there).
  2. Find knokkeroo TV and install it.
  3. Open it. It will display a 6-digit code on screen.

Step 3 · Pair the TV to your phone

This one does pair, unlike the Watch — the TV has no keyboard, so your phone authorises it.

  1. On your iPhone, open knokkeroo and make sure you are signed in.
  2. Go to Me → Settings → Pair a TV.
  3. Type the 6-digit code shown on the television.
  4. The TV screen moves on to your status screen. That is it — you only do this once.

Before you pair a shared or family TV: anyone who can see that screen can see your contacts' check-in status. Read the "Connected devices and wearables" clause in the Terms below first.

What to test

  • TV shows a 6-digit pairing code on first open
  • On the phone app (already signed in): Me → Pair a TV → enter the code
  • TV screen advances to the main status screen once paired
  • Opening the app is itself a check-in — the time under the kangaroo should come up green, not grey
  • Pressing the kangaroo records another knok (free — not gated behind a paid plan); the time turns green each time
  • Shared-screen awareness: anyone who can see the TV can see your contacts' check-in status — read the Terms' Connected Devices clause below before pairing a shared/communal TV

What this testing is for

The purpose of testing is to find errors. You are not here to confirm that knokkeroo works — you are here to find the places where it does not. A test that finds nothing tells us very little. A test that finds a problem is the most useful thing you can give us.

So please don't smooth anything over. If something looks wrong, reads oddly, arrives at a strange time, is hard to read, or simply confuses you, that counts — even if you're not sure it's a fault, and even if it feels too small to mention. Nothing is too small, and you can never report too much.

About the messages you receive

Every notification knokkeroo sends you during testing is part of the test. That includes reminders to knok, alerts that one of your contacts is overdue, invitations, and anything else that appears on your phone, watch or TV.

They're sent so we can confirm they actually arrive, on time, on your device. So please don't switch them off or ignore them — and do judge them as if they were real. If a message arrives that you didn't expect, arrives at an odd hour, says something confusing, or doesn't arrive when you think it should, that is exactly what we need to hear about.

knokkeroo is not an emergency service, and nothing you receive while testing should be treated as an emergency alert.

How to report anything you find

Two ways, whichever suits you:

  • WhatsApp — +27 84 600 1188
  • Email — hello@knokkeroo.com

Both go straight to Charles, who builds knokkeroo — there's no support desk or queue in between, and he reads every one.

Please include, as far as you can:

  • What you did — the steps, in the order you did them
  • What you expected to happen
  • What actually happened instead
  • When it happened — the date and roughly what time
  • Which device you were using
  • A screenshot, if there's anything to see

Don't worry about getting this perfect or writing it up neatly — a short message saying "this looked wrong at about 7 this morning" is far better than saying nothing. On WhatsApp a photo or a voice note is perfectly fine — you don't have to type it out.

If you're working from the downloadable test plan, put the test case ID in the subject line.
