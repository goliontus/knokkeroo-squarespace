⌚ Garmin

Connect IQ Store · closed — Garmin will not list knokkeroo

CLOSED — REJECTED BY GARMIN, 3 AUGUST 2026

knokkeroo watch companion — v1.1.1 (beta listing only)

Garmin declined to list knokkeroo publicly, and the stream is now closed. A public listing was submitted on 3 August and rejected the same day, about two hours later. Their reason, verbatim: "given the nature of the app, this is something we are unfortunately not able to approve for the store as it would fall under our 'safety awareness' category."

This is a category exclusion, not a defect — there is nothing to fix and resubmit. Garmin's own App Review Guidelines reserve rejection for apps that "may create a false sense of security, such as 'safety awareness' apps". Their worry is reliance: someone believing help is coming when the watch is off, out of range or the phone is flat. Our disclaimer was read, thanked for, and set aside in the same sentence. A developer who argued the same policy over an anchor alarm — which mirrored Garmin's own app — was blocked by their legal department and never reversed.

Decision taken: do not appeal, and do not send them the Terms. Our Terms describe a system that alerts your contacts when you are overdue, which argues Garmin's case rather than ours. Garmin's policy explicitly permits distribution outside their store, so the developer-account beta listing is a sanctioned route, not a workaround.

YOUR NEXT STEP: none. Nothing is owed here. The rejected public listing and the beta listing both still exist and were deliberately left in place — say the word if you want either removed. Pair a Garmin is now founder-only in the phone app (v4.37.0), so no ordinary user is offered a door that opens onto nothing, while your own watch keeps working exactly as it does today.

Live user counts on a LaMetric Time

Nothing was built for LaMetric and nothing was published to their app market. The route is LaMetric's own "My Data DIY" app, which you install on the clock and point at a URL we control — so there is no LaMetric app to write, submit or maintain.

How "and nobody else" is actually enforced, since a clock polling the open internet means a public URL: the address carries a secret, and anything without it gets a plain 404 rather than a "wrong password", so probing the address teaches an outsider nothing. The query asks the database for counts only — no names, no numbers, no rows — so even a leaked URL reveals nothing but totals. Demo accounts are excluded, or the number on the wall would be about ten too high.
