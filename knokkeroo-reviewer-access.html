Apple → iPhone

The main app. App Store id 6776208791, bundle id com.goliontus.knokker.

How to sign in — no SMS is sent, nothing can be charged.

  1. On the login screen open the country selector and choose United States (+1).
  2. Enter phone number 202 555 0100 and tap Send verification code.
  3. Enter code 148392 and tap Verify code.

A reserved demo account: the code is fixed, no message is ever sent, and it starts on the FREE plan so the paywall can be reviewed. Nine further demo accounts are listed in the reference section at the foot of this page.

What to test: sign in with the demo account above; allow notifications when the app asks (new in this version — see "New in version 4" below); tap Knok and watch your own time update; open the contacts list and check the status colours (green recently checked in, amber a little overdue, red missed); open a contact tile to see their current status, when they last checked in and how (new); set quiet hours; optionally add the home-screen widget (new, requires iOS 17 or later); then use Me → Delete account, which permanently removes the account.

What to review specifically — the paywall. The demo account starts FREE, so the first four contacts are visible and further contacts appear as anonymous locked tiles that open the paywall. Tapping Subscribe on a demo account is simulated: it unlocks everything with no transaction, no charge and no purchase sheet of any kind. Log out and back in to return to the free view and review it again. For real users, Subscribe opens Apple's own In-App Purchase sheet, which must be confirmed and can be cancelled. Nothing a reviewer taps can charge them.

Push notifications — see the "New in version 4" section for exactly what the app sends and when. Free accounts receive red overdue alerts and their own opt-in daily reminder; subscribers additionally receive amber early warnings, invitation notices and "checking in on you" requests. Demo accounts are exempt from every plan gate, so reviewers can test all of it with no purchase.

Please note: knokkeroo is a personal communication tool, not a medical or emergency service, and never contacts emergency services. The in-app disclaimer and the Terms state this.

✓ End of the iPhone review section — you have everything you need to complete it. Nothing further on this page is required.

Apple → Apple Watch

The knokkeroo Apple Watch app is part of the same iPhone app (App Store id 6776208791) — there is no separate App Store listing or TestFlight build to install. It installs automatically alongside the phone app onto any paired Apple Watch. The Watch companion ships from app version 4.38.0 — the build under review.

  1. Install and sign in to knokkeroo on the iPhone using the demo account in section 2 above.
  2. On the paired Apple Watch, open the Watch app (installed automatically — check the Watch's app list, or open the Watch app on iPhone and confirm knokkeroo shows as installed).
  3. Open knokkeroo on the Watch. It shows a single knok button.
  4. Tap knok on the Watch. Return to the iPhone app — the Me tile should update to reflect the new check-in.

Plan note: for real users the Apple Watch app is part of the paid plan (enforced server-side, not just in the UI). Demo accounts are exempt from every plan gate, so reviewing the Watch requires no purchase. Opening the Watch app itself counts as a check-in for the signed-in user, and tapping knok records another.

Apple → Apple TV ("knokkeroo TV")

knokkeroo TV is a separate app with its own App Store listing (App Store id 6795920763, bundle id com.goliontus.knokker.tv) and its own TestFlight build. It is a companion display only — it requires the iPhone app to be signed in first, then paired to it.

  1. Install knokkeroo on an iPhone or simulator. It is publicly available on the App Store (id 6776208791) — no TestFlight build is needed for this step. Sign in with the same demo account as section 2, repeated here so you need not scroll back: country United States (+1), phone 202 555 0100, code 148392. No SMS is sent.
  2. Install and open knokkeroo TV on the Apple TV / tvOS simulator. It displays a 6-digit pairing code.
  3. On the iPhone app, go to Me → Settings and choose Pair a TV, then enter the 6-digit code shown on the TV.
  4. The TV screen should then show the demo account's approved contacts and their check-in status tiles, matching what the phone app shows.

What to check specifically: the pairing flow above; that contact status tiles match the phone app; general Siri Remote navigation; and the app icon / Top Shelf image on the tvOS Home Screen.

Free feature: knokkeroo TV is free on every plan, same as the Watch app and widgets.

Google → Wear OS watch

The Wear OS app is the Android counterpart of the Apple Watch app above. It ships under the same package name as the phone app (com.goliontus.knokker) rather than as a separate listing, so Google Play delivers it to a paired watch alongside the phone app. Works on Galaxy Watch, Pixel Watch and other Wear OS devices.

  1. Install knokkeroo on an Android phone and sign in with the demo account from section 2, repeated here: country United States (+1), phone 202 555 0100, code 148392. No SMS is sent.
  2. On a Wear OS watch already paired to that phone, open knokkeroo. If it has not installed itself, open the Play Store on the watch → Apps on your phone → install knokkeroo.
  3. There is no pairing step and no login screen on the watch. The phone pushes its signed-in session to the watch over the Wearable Data Layer automatically, so simply opening the phone app once is sufficient. This is the Android equivalent of the Apple Watch's app-group session sharing.
  4. The watch shows the demo account's current status and a knok button. Tapping it records a check-in, which appears in the phone app without a manual refresh.

What to check specifically: that the watch opens already signed in; that a watch-recorded knok reaches the phone; and that a signed-out state is shown clearly rather than crashing (sign out on the phone, then reopen the watch app).

Free feature: the Wear OS app is free on every plan, same as the Apple Watch app, the TV app and widgets. It never opens a purchase sheet.

Reference — shared background

You do not need anything below to complete a review; every device section above is self-contained. This is here only if you want more detail.

What the app does

knokkeroo is a simple daily check-in app. A user taps knok to let the people who care about them know they are okay. Contacts watch over each other through mutual, both-approved connections and are alerted if someone is overdue.

It is a personal communication tool — not a medical or emergency service. Please see the in-app disclaimer and our Terms for the full emergency disclaimer.

New in version 5.0.10 (Android is live; iOS build 49 is the version under review)

  • Check-in requests pulse the knok button. When a contact asks the user to check in, the app no longer shows a message banner with its own button — the notification lands the user on the normal home screen and the knok button itself pulsates gently for ten seconds; one tap answers the request. Users with the reduce-motion accessibility setting get no animation (the request still lands on the home screen quietly). To verify with two demo accounts: from one account open a contact and tap "Ask them to check in", then watch the other device's knok button.
  • The knok button and widgets always show the user's own live status colour (green/amber/red), never a neutral brand colour.
  • Human error messages: no raw technical error text ever reaches a screen — connectivity problems say "check your connection", each message carries a short support code.
  • Widget freshness improvements and accessibility fixes (screen-reader labels on the consent screen, larger touch targets).

New in version 5.0.0 (the previous release, now live)

  • Android push notifications now work. Earlier version-4 Android builds shipped without Firebase configuration, so notification registration failed silently; 5.0.0 carries the full configuration. To verify: sign in with the demo account, allow notifications, then Me → Settings → "Send a test notification" — a banner should appear on the device within seconds.
  • iOS in-app purchases are fully functional. Earlier version-4 iOS builds shipped with a placeholder purchases key, so Subscribe showed "Coming soon"; 5.0.0 carries the production key. On a demo account Subscribe remains simulated (no sheet, no charge — so review is never blocked); on a real account it opens Apple's own In-App Purchase sheet.
  • Redesigned terms-acceptance screen: five numbered plain-language points, a full-width "Read the full Terms & Conditions" row, explicit accept and decline actions (declining does not enter the app).
  • Home-screen widget keeps its colour: the knok button retains the user's own green/amber/red status across widget refreshes instead of resetting to blue.
  • Reliability: provider-verified delivery logging behind the scenes, clearer error messages, and honest confirmation wording throughout (no message claims a delivery that has not been confirmed).

New in version 4 (vs the previous 3.5.x builds)

Version 4.38.0 is the first version 4 release submitted to the stores. Everything below is new relative to the 3.5.x builds previously reviewed.

Push notifications — new to this app entirely

The 3.5.x builds sent no push notifications at all. Version 4 introduces them, with deliberate limits:

  • Contact status alerts: if someone a user watches goes noticeably past their own chosen check-in interval, the watcher is notified — amber ("has a check-in update") at 1.5× the interval, red ("may need a check-in") at 2×. Users can choose amber-and-red or red-only, or turn these off.
  • Invitations: a notification when someone asks to connect, and when an invitation is accepted.
  • Daily reminder (opt-in): a single gentle reminder at a user-chosen time, only on days the user has not yet checked in.
  • "Checking in on you" requests: a watcher can tap "Ask them to check in" on a contact; the contact gets one notification saying who is asking.
  • Limits, enforced server-side: each recipient's own quiet hours are always respected; free accounts receive only red alerts and their own opt-in reminder (subscribers receive every type); at most one notification is dispatched per person per delivery cycle; all notifications are delivered silently (no sound). Tapping a notification opens the app on the relevant contact. A "checking in on you" request additionally offers a press-and-hold "knok now" action (from 4.39.8) that records the check-in without opening the app.
  • The app asks for the platform's standard notification permission after sign-in. Declining leaves the whole app functional.

Home-screen widgets and quick actions — new

  • Both platforms get a home-screen widget: the small size is the user's own knok button with their last check-in time; the larger size adds exactly one favourite contact the user explicitly picked via "Set favourite", shown with that contact's last check-in time (from 4.39.8, tapping the favourite opens their settings screen in the app via the knokkeroo:// URL scheme). The widget never shows phone numbers and never shows any contact the user did not pick. The iOS widget requires iOS 17 or later (the app itself runs on iOS 16.4+).
  • Long-pressing the app icon offers a quick action to knok without opening the app.
  • Opening the app now itself counts as a check-in, so simply launching knokkeroo reassures a user's watchers.

Companion apps — new

  • Apple Watch (inside the same iPhone app) and Wear OS (same Android package): one-tap knok from the wrist; opening the watch app counts as a check-in.
  • Apple TV ("knokkeroo TV", separate listing): a companion display paired from the phone.

Screen changes

  • Contact screen: now shows the contact's current status colour, when they last checked in and how (for example "by opening the app", from a widget, or from a watch), plus the "Ask them to check in" button.
  • Notification settings (Me → Settings): new controls for contact status alerts (amber-and-red / red-only / off), the opt-in daily reminder and its time, alongside the existing quiet hours.
  • In-app banner: the most recent notification is shown inside the app with a knok button next to it, so a notification tapped from the lock screen is never lost.
  • Paired devices: Me → Settings lists paired companion devices (for example a paired TV).

Plan gating (unchanged paywall, two new gated features)

The free tier still shows a user's first four contacts, with the paid subscription showing all of them — same as 3.5.x. Newly, setting up push notifications and the Apple Watch app are part of the paid plan for real users, enforced server-side. Widgets, quick actions, Wear OS and the TV app are free on every plan. Demo accounts are exempt from every plan gate, so reviewers can test everything with no purchase.

Suggested test steps (general)

  1. Contacts screen: after login you see a grid of tiles — green for recently checked in, amber for due, red for overdue, and some with a moon for quiet hours. Three tiles appear per row.
  2. knok: tap the knok tile. Your Me tile turns green and updates the time.
  3. Add a contact: tap Add Contact, then enter a name and a number. For demo accounts, no real message is sent.
  4. Respond to a request: tap a purple Approve tile to accept or decline a pending request.
  5. Timezones & quiet hours: the demo contacts are pinned to real timezones across the United States (Pacific, Mountain, Central, Eastern, Alaska) and Europe (London, Berlin, Athens). Each contact's time and quiet-hours moon reflect their own timezone, so they will not match your device's time. That is intentional, to show the feature. A normal, non-demo account instead follows the device's timezone.
  6. Settings: tap Me to change your name, knok interval, and quiet hours.
  7. Account deletion: open Me → Delete account. Demo accounts are protected and show a notice. Real accounts are permanently deleted. See our Account Deletion policy.

Notes for reviewers

  • No real SMS/WhatsApp is sent for demo accounts. Logging in and inviting demo contacts triggers no messages.
  • Demo data is reset by us between reviews, so the demo accounts always start in a known state.
  • Demo contacts keep fixed US/European timezones. We never overwrite them. Their displayed times differ from yours on purpose so that timezone and quiet-hours behaviour can be reviewed.
  • The app targets adults and is not directed at children.
  • Subscriptions & billing: the free tier fully shows a user's first four contacts; a paid subscription shows all of them. The subscription is sold and billed entirely by the platform's own store — Apple In-App Purchase on iOS and Google Play Billing on Android — and we never see or store card details. On the Huawei AppGallery build there is no billing at all: every contact is visible, free. All demo accounts start on the FREE plan. Tapping Subscribe on a demo account is simulated — it unlocks everything with no charge and no store purchase sheet at all; log out and back in to reset to the free view. For real (non-demo) users, Subscribe opens that platform's own cancellable purchase sheet. No purchase is required to review the app, and nothing a reviewer taps can charge them.
