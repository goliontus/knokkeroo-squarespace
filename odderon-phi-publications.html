The missing overlap

What marginal availability totals can and cannot tell us about usable learning time

Charles E. Joubert · Submitted to International Journal of Educational Development on 18 November 2026 · Under consideration

Studies of lost school time usually report each loss on its own: days the teacher is absent, lessons that are interrupted, days the pupil is away. Ministries and researchers then combine these totals into one figure for usable learning time, by adding the losses or by multiplying the remaining shares. Both steps assume how the losses coincide, which is rarely measured. This paper shows what the totals can and cannot establish. Usable time must lie between one minus the sum of the losses (or zero) and one minus the largest loss, and the totals cannot say where in that range it falls. We apply this to published sources. In a United States district study (Kraft and Novicoff 2024), adding and multiplying differ by less than 2% of allocated time, yet the totals alone allow a range 39% to 56% as wide as the published loss. In Nigerian survey data (Akseer and Játiva 2021), 29% of teachers report at least one form of recurrent absence, against 51% expected if the four forms were independent, and the share is below independence in all 32 reported subgroups: absence is concentrated on fewer teachers. Reports for Liberia, Ghana and secondary schools in Rwanda show the same direction in every subgroup whose printed figures are internally consistent. Two synthetic examples show that identical totals can call for opposite remedies for a learner. We close with a short observation protocol that records, for a stated task, which conditions were available together and which were missing together.

Granularity Invariance in Software-Test Measurement

Exact Bounds and a Study of Defects4J

Charles E. Joubert · Submitted to Empirical Software Engineering on 18 November 2026 · Under consideration

A reported test case is a container that a tester or framework chooses, and practitioners have long argued it is not a valid unit of measurement. This paper reports a sensitivity audit of how the reporting level changes test measurements when the evidence stays fixed. A suite is modelled as recorded test invocations plus a partition of them, and a metric is granularity invariant if the partition cannot change its value. Three elementary exact results bound what regrouping can do to pass rate, a coverage percentage over a chosen list, and an order by a count-normalised metric. All 854 active bugs of Defects4J 3.0.1 were screened: 794 gave a usable record (2,336,938 JUnit invocations), reported at invocation, method and class level, and 710 a mutation score. Between method and class level the pass-rate order of two bugs reverses in 23.72% of 314,821 pairs, mostly between nearly equal rates. Mutants killed per unit and mutation score order about half of 251,572 pairs oppositely at every level, and moving from method to class level changes that verdict in 13.29% of pairs.

Neutrino stars, thirty years on

The gravitational phase transition of heavy neutrino matter and what is now known about the neutrino masses

Charles E. Joubert · Submitted to The European Physical Journal H on 13 November 2026 · Under consideration

In 1996 Bilić and Viollier showed that a cooling gas of gravitating neutrinos with a mass of 10 to 25 keV condenses into degenerate “neutrino stars”. This paper revisits that model, the subject of my 1997–98 MSc thesis at the University of Cape Town, in the light of what is now known about the neutrino masses and the orbits of stars around Sgr A*. It evaluates the 1997 scales at today's mass limits, explains why such light neutrinos do not form a self-gravitating condensate, and shows that the same scaled equations reappear in the fermionic dark-matter cores fitted to the Galactic centre today. An independent 2026 solution reproduces the published transition temperature. The paper proposes no new physics.

Heavy neutrino matter at finite temperature

Charles Erasmus Joubert · MSc, Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, University of Cape Town, 1998

A study of the phase transition linked to gravitational collapse in a system of massive fermions that interact only through gravity. Cooling a non-degenerate gas of massive neutrinos below a critical temperature produces a condensed phase of quasi-degenerate supermassive neutrino stars, compact dark objects that may have seeded galactic nuclei and quasars. The preprint above revisits this work.
