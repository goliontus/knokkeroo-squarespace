6. How to request access to a record

The short version: complete Form 2 of the PAIA Regulations, email it to hello@odderonphi.com, pay the R140 request fee if one is payable, and we will respond within 30 days.

  1. Complete the prescribed Form 2 (Request for Access to Record of Private Body), published in the PAIA Regulations, 2021 and available from the Information Regulator's website.
  2. Provide enough detail to identify the record and to identify you, state the right you are seeking to exercise or protect and why the record is required to exercise or protect it, and state the form of access and the manner of reply you prefer.
  3. If you are requesting on behalf of someone else, attach proof of your authority in a form acceptable to the Information Officer.
  4. Send the form to hello@odderonphi.com or to the postal address in section 1.
  5. Pay the request fee where one is payable. A requester asking for their own personal information does not pay a request fee.

We will decide within 30 days of receiving the request and notify you in writing. That period may be extended by up to a further 30 days where PAIA allows it, in which case we will tell you why. If the record contains information about a third party, we must first give that party an opportunity to make representations, which may extend the timeline as provided in PAIA.

A request may be made in a different way where a requester cannot read or write, or has a disability — contact the Information Officer and reasonable assistance will be provided.

We will tell you what the fees are before doing the work, and access is given once the fees are paid. Where a fee is payable and you dispute it, the remedies in section 9 apply.

8. Grounds on which access may be refused

PAIA obliges or permits a private body to refuse access in the circumstances set out in Chapter 4 of Part 3 of the Act. The grounds most likely to arise here are:

  • Section 63 — unreasonable disclosure of personal information about a third party, including a deceased person.
  • Section 64 — commercial information of a third party, such as trade secrets, or information whose disclosure would harm their commercial or financial interests.
  • Section 65 — information supplied in confidence, where disclosure would be a breach of a duty of confidence.
  • Section 66 — information whose disclosure could endanger a person's life or physical safety, or prejudice the security of property or a system.
  • Section 67 — privileged records in legal proceedings.
  • Section 68 — the company's own commercial information, including trade secrets, source code and information whose disclosure would put us at a disadvantage in negotiations or commercially.
  • Section 69 — research information of the company or a third party.
  • Section 70 — the public-interest override, which requires disclosure despite a ground of refusal where the public interest in disclosure clearly outweighs the harm.

Where part of a record may be refused and the rest may not, we will sever the record and give you access to the remainder, as section 28 requires.

9. If we refuse your request

There is no internal appeal against a decision of a private body. If your request is refused, or you are unhappy with the fee, the form of access, or a failure to respond in time, you may:

  • Complain to the Information Regulator in terms of section 77A of PAIA, using Form 5, within 180 days of the decision — PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za.
  • Apply to a court with jurisdiction, in terms of section 78 of PAIA, for appropriate relief.

10. Your rights over your personal information

Separately from PAIA, POPIA gives you rights over personal information the company holds about you. You may:

  • Ask us to confirm, free of charge, whether we hold personal information about you, and to give you a record or description of it.
  • Ask us to correct or delete personal information that is inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading or obtained unlawfully, using Form 2 of the POPIA Regulations.
  • Object to processing on the grounds set out in section 11(3) of POPIA, using Form 1 of the POPIA Regulations.
  • Withdraw consent where processing is based on consent, without affecting processing that already took place.
  • Ask us not to use your details for direct marketing, and be told the source of your details where we did not obtain them from you.
  • Complain to the Information Regulator at complaints.IR@inforegulator.org.za, or institute civil proceedings.

For a knokkeroo account, deletion is quickest in the app itself — see knokkeroo Account Deletion. Because a knokkeroo account is tied to a mobile phone number, we verify that a requester controls the number or is properly authorised before we act on a request; a request that we cannot verify may be refused, delayed or restricted.
